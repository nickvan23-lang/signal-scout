Here’s how to use it.

No Bluetooth signals appear

  1. Check Bluetooth. Make sure it is enabled in iPhone Settings.
  2. Check app permission. Open Settings → Privacy & Security → Bluetooth and allow Signal Scout. Return to the app.
  3. Wake an accessory you own. Bring it near the phone. It must actively advertise Bluetooth Low Energy; sleeping, powered-off, connected, and closed-case accessories may be silent.
  4. Check for a paused scan. Resume scanning if the screen says it is paused.

Not every Bluetooth device can appear. Signal Scout cannot discover Wi-Fi clients, silent devices, or every Classic Bluetooth accessory. iOS controls which advertisements apps can receive.

How do I identify my accessory?

Start with the device’s advertised Bluetooth name and manufacturer. Names may be generic or missing; manufacturer data identifies a registered company but is not authenticated proof of a device’s maker or owner. Other nearby accessories may appear.

If you can move an accessory you control, bring it close to your iPhone and compare the signal that becomes stronger. Keep the phone still while making that comparison. A strong signal by itself does not prove which item it belongs to.

If the accessory is already lost and you cannot make a controlled comparison, treat each signal as a possible clue. Do not treat a broadcast name as verified identity.

How to read RSSI and the signal map

RSSI is received signal strength, measured in dBm. A reading of −50 dBm is stronger than −80 dBm. This is not a direct measurement of physical distance.

Dots closer to the map center represent stronger signals. Angles are stable visual positions used to separate dots; they are not compass bearings or walking directions.

Select a signal, hold the phone the same way, and take a few slow steps. Pause to collect fresh readings before comparing. Walls, people, reflections, orientation, and transmit power can change the result. Use the comparison reset when testing another position.

A signal disappeared or stopped changing

The accessory may have stopped advertising or moved out of range. Wake it when possible, return toward your last position, and watch for new readings. A last-seen time or stale reading is historical data, not proof the accessory is still nearby.

Pausing the scanner intentionally stops new readings. Resume before interpreting another trend. Inactive signals can be cleared from Scanner options; the app also removes old unselected records.

Can it find headphones, earbuds, or a powered-off device?

Headphones and earbuds may appear only while actively advertising BLE. Some stop advertising in a closed case, while asleep, or after connecting. Visibility varies by model and state. A powered-off item cannot broadcast a signal for Signal Scout to measure.

Use an accessory maker’s own finding service when available. Signal Scout does not provide a finding network, historical location, exact distance, or verified device identity.

Free access and older test builds

The submitted free release opens directly to the scanner. All features are free, without a subscription or time limit. There is no purchase or restore step. App Store review is still in progress.

If an earlier test build shows a subscription screen, it is not the current free release. Any existing Apple subscription must be managed separately through Apple’s subscription settings; installing a free build does not cancel an existing subscription.
