What do Bluetooth RSSI and dBm readings mean?

By Nicholas Alan Vandervelden · November 6, 2026

RSSI means received signal strength indicator. Signal Scout displays Bluetooth signal strength in dBm: −50 dBm is stronger than −80 dBm. Use changes from the same accessory as a search clue. A signal-strength reading is not an exact distance, compass direction, or confirmed device identity.

How to read negative dBm values

A value closer to zero is stronger. For example, −50 is greater than −80, so −50 dBm represents a stronger received signal. Compare the same accessory over time while keeping your phone’s orientation as consistent as possible.

Illustrative readings, not distance estimates

−45 dBmStronger than the other two example readings.

−85 dBmWeaker than the other two example readings.

These are examples, not fixed quality bands or a conversion to feet or meters. Different accessories use different transmit powers, so the strongest device in a list is not necessarily the closest physical object.

Why does the signal change while I stand still?

Walls, reflections, people, phone orientation, and accessory transmit power can affect received strength. A single reading can change without proving that you moved toward or away from the item. Signal Scout smooths readings and shows recent history to make a comparison easier to follow.

Look for a repeated trend across fresh observations instead of reacting to one jump. If conditions change, begin another comparison before drawing a conclusion.

What does the Signal Scout map show?

Dots nearer the center represent stronger signals. Their angles are visual spacing, not compass bearings. A dot on the right does not tell you to walk right, and a centered dot does not establish a measured distance.

Use the map to compare signal strengths, or select a candidate from the device list to inspect its trend. Names and advertised manufacturers can help choose a candidate, but broadcast details do not prove identity or ownership.

A useful comparison routine

  1. Select a possible match and check that its readings are fresh.
  2. Hold your iPhone the same way and move a few slow steps.
  3. Stop walking while leaving the scanner running, then wait for more readings.
  4. Compare the recent trend with the previous position.
  5. Use Reset comparison when testing another position, and repeat one change at a time.

“Warmer” and “colder” describe the signal trend. They do not provide turn-by-turn navigation. If no consistent trend emerges, test another position or consider whether you selected the wrong candidate.

What if the reading is paused or lost?

A paused scanner is not collecting new observations. Choose Resume scanning before interpreting a new trend. A lost signal may mean the accessory stopped advertising or moved out of range. An old value only describes an earlier observation.

If the accessory no longer appears, use the missing-device checklist. No RSSI calculation can locate a powered-off device that is not broadcasting.
