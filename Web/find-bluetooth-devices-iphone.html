How to find nearby Bluetooth devices with an iPhone

By Nicholas Alan Vandervelden · November 6, 2026

Use a Bluetooth Low Energy finder to scan for a nearby accessory that is still broadcasting, select a possible match, and compare fresh signal-strength readings as you move. Signal Scout is a free iPhone app for this process. A silent or powered-off accessory cannot appear.

Before you search

Signal Scout runs on iPhone with iOS 17.0 or later. The accessory needs power, must be actively advertising Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), and must be within range of the phone. iOS must also make its advertisements available to apps. A device that appears in your Bluetooth pairing settings will not necessarily appear in a BLE scanner.

Search for an accessory you own or have permission to locate. If you can access a similar accessory you control, use it to learn how the readings change before starting a difficult search.

1. Open Signal Scout and allow Bluetooth

Open the app and allow its Bluetooth permission. If permission was previously denied, check Settings → Privacy & Security → Bluetooth and enable Signal Scout. The app opens to the scanner without an account or subscription.

If scanning is paused, choose Resume scanning. Walking and pausing your movement is different from pausing the scanner: keep scanning active when collecting new readings.

2. Choose a possible match

Look at the device list for an advertised name and manufacturer. Some accessories broadcast a generic name or no name. Manufacturer information can be missing too. These broadcast fields help you choose a candidate; they do not authenticate a device or identify its owner.

Select a signal to examine its readings. A stronger signal alone does not prove that it is your missing item.

3. Move slowly, stop, and compare

Keep the phone oriented the same way, move a few steps, and stop walking while the scanner collects fresh readings. Compare the smoothed trend with your previous position. Try one change at a time so you can tell which movement affected the signal.

For example, if the same accessory reads around −75 dBm at one position and around −60 dBm at another, the second signal is stronger. Those numbers do not establish an exact distance. Walls, people, reflections, orientation, and transmit power can affect the comparison.

4. Start a new comparison when needed

Use Reset comparison when trying a new position. Watch the fresh, paused, or lost status before interpreting the trend. If a signal disappears, return toward the last position where you received fresh readings. A last-seen value is historical information, not proof the item is still there.

Can this find lost headphones or earbuds?

Only if they are actively advertising BLE and visible to iOS. Some accessories stop advertising when connected, asleep, or inside a closed case. Opening a case can help when you can access it; an app cannot wake a silent, missing accessory by measuring a signal that is not there.

Check the accessory maker’s own finding service for any supported network or last-known-location features. Signal Scout measures nearby signals and does not replace those services.
